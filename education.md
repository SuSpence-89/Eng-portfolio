# Chapter 2: The Long Road Through Education

### From Homeschooling to Engineering Technology — And Beyond

If there's one thing I can say about my educational journey, it's that I certainly didn't take the conventional route.

My path through education has included false starts, interruptions, unexpected changes, and quite a few occasions when I had to reconsider what I wanted from life. But somewhere along the way, something changed: education went from something I needed to finish into something I genuinely wanted to pursue.

## My First Experience With Structured Education

As I mentioned in my childhood story, my siblings and I were homeschooled. My early education was unconventional, and I didn't learn to read until I was around 12 years old.

Sometime between the ages of 11 and 15, our homeschooling took a different direction. Our family enrolled in a distance education program, and we were supplied with a home computer to complete our coursework. This was back in the days of **Windows 95 and Windows 98**.

For the first time, I had something resembling a structured learning environment. There were assignments, tests, deadlines, and expectations. Much of the learning was still self-directed, but there was a system in place to guide us. I remember jumping from approximately Grade 3 mathematics to Grade 8 material.

We participated for about a year, and I still remember how kind the program coordinator was. Until then, I'd only really experienced my mother's teaching style. And while I love my mom, teaching eight children wasn't exactly her greatest strength. Sorry, Mom!

The program even provided an opportunity for a road trip. We visited the **Calgary Zoo** and stopped in **Sparwood, British Columbia**. I hope to dig up some photographs from that trip someday.

## Entering Public School — A Whole New World

Eventually, my twin sisters and I enrolled at **Boundary Central Secondary School (BCSS)** in Midway, British Columbia.

This was a pivotal change in my life. Almost everything I'd known revolved around homeschooling, the family farm, and my brothers and sisters. Outside my family, I'd had a pen pal, but very little experience with the kind of everyday social interaction other children grew up with.

Suddenly I was surrounded by people my own age, learning not only school subjects but also the unwritten social rules most students had learned years earlier. Fortunately, I was a big, strong kid, so I didn't encounter much direct bullying. Despite the steep learning curve, **I genuinely enjoyed high school**.

I was placed in Grade 9 with my twin sisters. Later I learned that I should have been in Grade 10, but Mom had kept us together so we'd share classes. I was upset when I found out, particularly because many of the kids I felt most comfortable with were in Grade 10.

Still, BCSS opened up a world beyond the farm and my immediate family.

## Leaving School and Joining the Military

I attended high school until Grade 11. A personal family event involving my father led to me moving out of the family home. I moved in with my girlfriend at the time, began working full-time, and took a couple of Grade 12 courses through correspondence.

When I turned 18 in **2008**, I enlisted in the Canadian Armed Forces as an **Aviation Systems Technician**. At the time, the occupation only required Grade 10, so I qualified.

I went through the military training system and earned top marks during my technical training in **Borden, Ontario**. My first posting was to **Comox, British Columbia**.

## Returning to School in Comox

While posted to Comox, I decided I should probably finish high school. I enrolled in academic upgrading through **North Island College (NIC)** in nearby Courtenay. I completed my Grade 12 English and began working on mathematics.

Then life intervened again. I was posted to **Cold Lake, Alberta**, and my education was interrupted once more. At the time I still expected to have a military career ahead of me, so graduating remained something I planned to do eventually.

I didn't yet realize how important it was about to become.

## November 2015 — An Unexpected Turning Point

In **November 2015**, I was diagnosed with **Type 1 diabetes**. The diagnosis changed my life in ways I hadn't anticipated, including learning that my military career was coming to an end.

Suddenly I had to think seriously about my future outside the Armed Forces. And there was an uncomfortable reality: despite years of military technical training and experience as an aviation technician, **I still didn't have a high school diploma**.

Finishing school was no longer something I could put off. I needed to do it for myself, for my wife, and for the family we hoped to build.

## Finishing High School — On My Own Terms

Between **2015 and 2019**, I worked toward completing my remaining high school requirements online while continuing to work full-time, adjusting to my diagnosis, and attending medical appointments. In early 2018 my wife became pregnant, and we had even more responsibilities to balance.

I deliberately chose **not to pursue a GED**. I had already completed English and Social Studies. What I still needed was mathematics, physics, and chemistry.

By then I'd decided I wanted to become a **Mechanical Engineering Technologist**. Rather than looking for the easiest way to graduate, I completed the more demanding science and math courses that would prepare me for engineering technology studies.

I finally received my **high school diploma in 2020**.

It had taken me considerably longer than most people, but I'd finished what I'd started. And by that point, graduating no longer felt like the finish line. It felt like a starting point.

## Lakeland College — Beginning Post-Secondary Education

After earning my high school diploma, I enrolled in **Lakeland College's Renewable Energy & Conservation** program. Renewable energy genuinely interested me, and I was excited to begin post-secondary education.

In **2021**, I finished my first year and earned a certificate. I began working toward the second year and the diploma, but my military transition meant that I had to change plans and begin my **Mechanical Engineering Technology** program at NAIT.

I fully intended to return and finish what I'd started.

## NAIT — Becoming a Mechanical Engineering Technologist

Attending **NAIT** was a major milestone. After years of juggling military service, family responsibilities, medical challenges, and academic upgrading, I was finally studying the field I'd worked toward: **Mechanical Engineering Technology**.

I successfully graduated and soon secured a job as a **Project Coordinator**, my first civilian position after the military.

It was an important transition. I'd spent much of my adult life in uniform, and now I was beginning a new career with the education I'd worked so hard to obtain.

## Finishing What I Started

I hadn't forgotten my unfinished renewable energy studies. In **2024**, I returned to Lakeland online. The program had since changed its name to **Sustainable Energy Technology**, but the goal I'd set myself was the same.

In **2025**, I completed the diploma. I now held two post-secondary diplomas in fields I was genuinely interested in: **Mechanical Engineering Technology** and **Sustainable Energy Technology**.

## Earning My C.E.T. Designation

I continued working toward professional recognition as an engineering technologist. In **October 2026**, I earned my **Certified Engineering Technologist (C.E.T.)** designation through the Association of Science and Engineering Technology Professionals of Alberta (**ASET**).

That was another meaningful milestone in a journey that began under some rather unusual circumstances.

But somewhere along the way, my relationship with education had fundamentally changed.

## A Fire in My Belly — Discovering a Thirst for Knowledge

There's something I find particularly interesting when I look back at the person I was in 2015 compared with who I am today.

**I barely recognize that young man anymore.**

Back then, life was forcing me to reconsider my future. I needed to finish school, find a new career, and figure out how to provide for my family. At first, education was something I needed to accomplish so I could move forward.

But at some point, especially after finally earning my high school diploma, **a fire was lit in my belly**.

I developed a genuine thirst for knowledge.

I wasn't simply studying because I needed another qualification. I wanted to learn. The more I studied, the more I realized how much there was that I didn't know. Rather than discouraging me, that realization excited me.

Engineering, science, energy systems, and technology opened up an enormous world of subjects I wanted to understand more deeply. My diplomas and C.E.T. designation were milestones worth celebrating, but they didn't make me feel as though I'd reached the end of my education.

Quite the opposite.

## The Next Frontier — A Degree in Energy Engineering

One of my long-term ambitions is to attend the **University of Calgary's Bachelor of Science in Energy Engineering** program through its **Schulich School of Engineering**.

What appeals to me about Energy Engineering is the way it draws from multiple disciplines, including mechanical, chemical, petroleum, and energy and environmental engineering. I've always enjoyed understanding how different systems interact, so the interdisciplinary nature of the program really interests me.

It's also a field evolving alongside major changes in the way the world produces, distributes, stores, and consumes energy. Solar and wind generation are expanding, while energy storage, electrical grids, and electrification are creating new engineering challenges.

How do we produce energy more efficiently? How do we store it? How do we integrate different energy sources into existing systems? And how do we create practical, reliable solutions for places with very different environments and needs?

These are the kinds of questions I'd love to explore.

The possibility of **working internationally** appeals to me as well. Renewable energy is a global field, and I'd love the chance to combine my technical knowledge with the experience of living and working overseas someday.

I'm aware that returning to university would be a major commitment, especially while balancing work and family life. I don't have a timeline for it yet, and I'd need to confirm the admission pathway. But it's a goal I'd genuinely like to pursue.

**Ten years ago, I was worried about finishing high school. Today, I'm thinking about earning an engineering degree.**

That's a change I never would have predicted.

## Looking Back — And Looking Ahead

I went from homeschooling on a rural farm to correspondence lessons on an old Windows computer. I entered public school with little social experience, left before graduating, joined the military, and spent years working toward an education I'd never properly finished.

I didn't receive my high school diploma until **2020**. But I eventually earned two post-secondary diplomas and my C.E.T. designation.

And somehow, after all that, I still want to go back to school!

I enjoy challenging myself, exploring unfamiliar subjects, and discovering how much more there is to understand. Maybe one day I'll walk across a stage at the University of Calgary to accept an engineering degree. I don't know exactly when or whether that will happen, but I like having something to work toward.

**My educational journey may have started later than most, but I don't think it's finished yet.**
