Some friendships begin in the most unexpected ways. Mine with Ryan began when we were just kids, probably around six or eight years old, growing up in Rock Creek, British Columbia.

My dad and Ryan's grandfather knew each other quite well. I believe they may have both been ironworkers, although I can't remember for certain. What I do remember is that one day Ryan's grandfather brought him over to our family property, and the two of us hit it off almost immediately.

We were kids. We played, had fun, and got into whatever harmless trouble two young boys could find. But there's one particular memory from that day that has somehow stayed with me all these years.

The ghost bike.

I was pretty proud of the fact that I could ride my bicycle without training wheels, and naturally I wanted to show Ryan. We were riding up and down our rough, unpaved driveway, which had a decent hill along it.

At some point, Ryan asked if I wanted to see a ghost bike.

A ghost bike? What the hell was a ghost bike?

Well, Ryan was more than happy to demonstrate. He grabbed my bicycle, started running alongside it down the hill, and then simply let go. And there it went. My bicycle continued rolling down the driveway, completely on its own.

To my six- or eight-year-old brain, this was absolutely fascinating. A bicycle riding itself! I thought it was the coolest thing imaginable, and I wouldn't be surprised if we repeated the trick several times that afternoon.

Looking back as an adult, however, that little motherfucker basically grabbed my bike and threw it down a hill.

But at the time? Pure magic.

Oddly enough, that ridiculous little stunt would become important many years later.

After that childhood visit, life carried on. Ryan went his way, and I went mine. We didn't grow up regularly visiting each other, and for years I had no real connection with him. He was simply a kid who had come over with his grandfather one afternoon.

Eventually, I grew older and entered public school, joining my sisters in Grade 9. It was a big change for me, having been homeschooled growing up in Rock Creek. Suddenly I was surrounded by classmates, teachers, and the strange social world of high school.

Among the people I met was an interesting fellow named Ryan. He had long hair, loved heavy metal, and was a bit of an oddball. Naturally, we got along.

We started spending time together, particularly during lunch hours and breaks. Ryan was actually the same age as me, but he was in Grade 10 while I had been placed in Grade 9 with my sisters. That annoyed me, because it meant we didn't share classes. Nevertheless, we made the most of whatever time we had.

Then one day, while we were riding the school bus, Ryan brought up something completely unexpected. Apparently, he remembered me. Not just from high school, but from when we were little kids.

I had absolutely no idea what he was talking about. He seemed almost surprised that I didn't remember him. He started bringing up different memories, trying to jog my memory and convince me that we'd met before. Nothing was clicking.

Until he mentioned a ghost bike.

Holy shit. You're THAT Ryan!

Suddenly everything came flooding back. The driveway. The hill. My bicycle rolling away by itself. That little bastard from all those years ago was sitting right there beside me.

It was one of those moments when a seemingly insignificant childhood memory suddenly becomes something much bigger. And just like that, an old friendship was rediscovered. From that point onward, Ryan became one of the most important people in my life.

The High School Years

A lot of our friendship developed during the seemingly ordinary moments of high school.

One of our favourite places to hang out was our English teacher's classroom. I believe his name was Mr. Anderson. He was kind enough to let a bunch of us spend our lunch hours there, provided we weren't being complete hooligans or making a mess of the place.

It became something of a gathering spot for all the weirdos, metalheads, and oddballs.

We had access to the computers, and practically every lunch hour somebody had AC/DC or some other rock or metal band blasting away. LimeWire was a big thing back then, so naturally there were plenty of conversations about music, downloads, and whatever songs we'd discovered.

We talked about everything. Life, love, music, girls, and whatever other nonsense happened to be occupying our teenage minds.

And then there was the gnome.

For some inexplicable reason, there was a garden gnome sitting on a ledge roughly ten feet above the floor. Of course, students noticed it. And, naturally, they asked Mr. Anderson about it.

What's with the gnome?

The funny thing was that he simply would not acknowledge its existence. It didn't matter how many times someone asked. As far as he was concerned, there was apparently no gnome worth discussing.

This became an ongoing joke between the students and our teacher. Looking back, it's such a ridiculous little detail, but those were the things that made high school memorable.

And throughout it all, there was Ryan. We could exchange a look across the room and immediately know what the other was thinking. We'd laugh at each other's stupid jokes, often without needing to explain why something was funny.

We just got each other. We were thick as thieves.

Life, Love, and Teenage Advice

Of course, being teenage boys, girls were a frequent topic of conversation.

I remember Ryan having a particular crush on a girl named Janelle. He thought she was gorgeous, and I certainly wasn't going to argue with him on that point.

The problem was that Ryan couldn't bring himself to ask her out. He was afraid of rejection.

Now, I've always had a certain philosophy about uncertainty. I don't particularly enjoy sitting around wondering what might happen when there's a perfectly reasonable way to find out. In my mind, there were two possibilities. She'd say yes, or she'd say no. Either way, at least he'd know.

For weeks I encouraged him to take the chance. I reminded him that he was a good-looking guy, that he was funny, and that he had nothing to gain by spending the rest of his life wondering what might have happened.

Eventually, he worked up the courage. Unfortunately, she turned him down. From what I remember, she was quite polite about it, but naturally Ryan was disappointed.

I tried to be there for him afterward. My reasoning was simple: at least he had his answer. He no longer had to waste his time wondering whether she might be interested.

Besides, for fuck's sake, there were about seven other girls he liked!

I don't actually know how Ryan looks back on that particular experience today. Perhaps he remembers it differently. But I'd like to think I was being the kind of friend who encouraged him to take chances, even when the outcome wasn't guaranteed.

Sometimes friendship isn't about telling someone what they want to hear. Sometimes it's about giving them the push they need, and being there afterward regardless of how things turn out.

Speaking of girls, Ryan also happened to have a thing for my sisters. I always found that rather hilarious. Part of me naturally had that protective brother reaction of, Hey, that's my sister!

But another part of me thought that, if one of my sisters was going to date somebody, Ryan certainly wouldn't have been a bad choice. I think he was particularly interested in Fiona.

Unfortunately for him, my sisters never really saw him that way. As far as they were concerned, he was simply Spencer's friend Ryan. And that was that.

Guitar Lessons, or How to Learn Everything Except the Guitar

Ryan was a musician. He played guitar, had several electric guitars and, if I remember correctly, an acoustic as well. He was even in a band.

I'd always been interested in learning an instrument, particularly the guitar. And who better to teach me than my best friend?

By that point I was old enough to have my learner's licence, or perhaps I'd already gotten my driver's licence. Either way, I was able to visit Ryan at his grandparents' place, where he had his space down in the basement.

That's where my guitar education began. Or at least, where it attempted to begin.

Ryan genuinely knew his stuff. He understood the instrument, the music, and the theory behind what he was playing. Unfortunately, we had two very different ideas about what constituted a guitar lesson.

He would demonstrate something, hand me the guitar, and let me pick away at the strings for a little while. Then we'd start talking. And before long, Ryan would launch into some lengthy explanation about musical theory, how things worked, why they worked, reading music, or some other fascinating technical aspect of the instrument.

Now, don't get me wrong. That stuff is interesting, and I could appreciate his enthusiasm. But I had a rather simpler objective.

I just wanted to learn how to play the damn guitar.

Give me a few chords, show me a basic strumming pattern, and let me practise until I could play something recognizable around a campfire. Instead, I felt like I was receiving a university lecture on music theory before I'd even learned how to strum properly.

I tried not to let my frustration show too much, although I did occasionally mention that I wanted to spend more time actually playing. Internally, however, I was thinking:

Ryan, you motherfucker, stop explaining the theory and just show me how to play the damn thing!

Eventually, for reasons I can't quite remember, the lessons stopped. I never did become much of a guitarist. I've since come to accept that musical talent isn't exactly one of my natural gifts.

But here's the funny thing. Even today, all these years later, I can still remember a couple of the strums Ryan taught me.

For all the talking, the theory, and my frustration, some of those practical lessons actually stuck. And I think that's pretty cool. I only wish we'd spent a little more time playing and a little less time discussing why the music worked.

A Friendship That Lasted

Looking back, I realize that much of high school has become a blur. There are undoubtedly countless moments with Ryan that I've forgotten, along with stories that will probably come back to me when something unexpectedly jogs my memory.

But what I remember most clearly isn't necessarily any one event. It's the feeling of having somebody who understood me. Someone who shared my sense of humour. Someone I could laugh with, have ridiculous conversations with, talk about serious things with, and simply enjoy being around.

Ryan and I were very different people in some respects, but somehow we clicked. And that friendship didn't end when high school did.

All these years later, Ryan is still my best friend. We still spend time together, still laugh, and still make new memories.

There are many more stories to tell about our friendship, particularly everything that happened as we grew older and our lives took different directions.

But for now, I can't help thinking about how it all began. Two little kids on a rough driveway in Rock Creek, one proudly showing off his bicycle, and the other deciding to demonstrate something called a ghost bike.

Who would have thought that years later, a passing mention of that ridiculous stunt on a school bus would reconnect two childhood friends?

And who would have imagined that the kid who effectively threw my bicycle down a hill would become my best friend for life?

Life's funny that way. And knowing Ryan, I'm sure we still have plenty of ridiculous stories left to make.
