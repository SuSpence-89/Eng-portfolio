## Project Overview

I independently designed and constructed a **custom modular home bar using reclaimed wood**, combining practical woodworking, mechanical assembly, glass fabrication, and decorative finishing techniques.

Although I drew visual inspiration from photographs of existing home bars, I developed my own construction methods, structural layout, fastening arrangements, and finishing processes.

Two primary design requirements guided the project from the beginning:

1. **Structural stability:** The completed bar needed to be sturdy and functional during everyday use.
2. **Modularity:** The major components needed to be removable so the bar could be disassembled into manageable sections for transportation or relocation.

Rather than constructing the entire bar as a permanently bonded structure, I designed individual assemblies that could be mechanically fastened together while maintaining a finished, furniture-quality appearance.

## 1. Material Sourcing & Reuse

The primary construction material originated from **wooden LUU-2D/B storage crates** encountered during my Royal Canadian Air Force service while working in the X-Area during an Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) assignment.

I retained the timber for future personal woodworking projects.

Material reclamation has always been an important part of my approach to fabrication. Whenever practical, I prefer to reuse existing wood rather than purchase new lumber.

I regularly collect and store usable material, considering its potential applications in future projects.

For this particular build, the reclaimed boards provided the foundation for a functional piece of furniture.

Repurposing the wood offered several advantages:

- Reduced the need to purchase new construction material.
- Extended the useful life of previously used timber.
- Encouraged resourceful design around available materials.
- Provided an opportunity to transform salvaged material into finished furniture.

This project demonstrates how material reuse can be incorporated into practical fabrication without sacrificing functionality or appearance.

## 2. Design Objectives & Planning

Rather than following a predetermined set of construction plans, I developed the bar's layout and assembly method independently.

The design needed to accommodate several functional elements:

- A rigid structural foundation.
- Two side storage compartments.
- A removable central wine-bottle organizer.
- A continuous wooden countertop.
- Glass shelving for bottles and accessories.
- Custom-cut mirrored backing.
- Overhead storage for inverted stemware.
- Concealed fasteners and decorative trim.

A major consideration was ensuring that the finished bar would not appear modular.

Although the structure could be disassembled, I wanted the completed product to look like a conventional piece of finished furniture.

I therefore incorporated concealed screw connections, removable covers, and decorative moulding to hide the mechanical fastening arrangements.

## 3. Structural Base & Modular Construction

The structural base was constructed using a combination of **wood screws and adhesive**.

These permanent connections provided rigidity and established a stable foundation for the remaining components.

Rather than making every individual joint removable, I treated the base as a single structural module.

The other major sections were connected using removable mechanical fasteners.

### Removable Side Compartments

The side compartments were secured using screws.

To maintain a clean external appearance, the fastening locations were concealed behind removable covers.

These covers allowed access to the screws when disassembly was required.

### Removable Wine-Bottle Storage

The central wine-bottle organizer was fabricated as a separate assembly.

It was retained using only a small number of screws, allowing the entire section to be withdrawn independently of the surrounding structure.

This approach simplified potential disassembly and provided flexibility for transportation.

### Removable Countertop

The countertop was secured using mechanical fasteners rather than being permanently bonded to the other sections.

This allowed it to be separated from the main structure if the bar needed to be dismantled.

The combined approach provided a balance between **structural rigidity, modular construction, and practical serviceability**.

## 4. Concealed Fasteners & Decorative Moulding

One of my design objectives was to conceal the mechanical connections required for modular construction.

The side-compartment fasteners were positioned behind removable covers.

For the countertop connections, I incorporated decorative moulding to conceal the underlying screws.

The selected trim was:

**Alexandria Moulding — Egg & Dart Decorative Basswood Moulding**

- Nominal dimensions: 5/8 in × 1 in.
- Supplied length: 8 ft.
- Material: Basswood.
- Profile: Decorative egg-and-dart pattern.

The moulding served two purposes.

First, it improved the finished appearance by introducing decorative detail along the woodwork.

Second, it concealed the underlying fastening arrangement, allowing the countertop to retain its finished appearance while remaining removable when necessary.

This demonstrated an important design principle:

**Functional mechanical connections can be incorporated into a finished product without compromising its appearance.**

## 5. Custom-Cut Mirrors & Removable Installation

To improve the appearance and functionality of the bar, I incorporated mirrored panels into the interior.

Rather than purchasing mirrors manufactured to predetermined sizes, I measured the required openings and used **glass cutters to produce custom-sized mirror panels**.

The mirrors provided a reflective backdrop for the displayed glassware and helped create a greater sense of depth within the compartments.

### Removable Mirror Mounting

Consistent with the modular construction philosophy, I deliberately avoided permanently bonding the mirrors to the wooden structure.

Instead, I used **hook-and-loop (Velcro) fastening** to secure the panels.

This approach allowed the mirrors to be removed if the bar needed to be:

- Disassembled for transportation.
- Repaired or modified.
- Reconfigured.
- Maintained or cleaned behind the panels.

The removable installation method also avoided making the glass panels permanent structural elements.

This was another example of designing components with future removal and accessibility in mind.

## 6. Routed Stemware Storage

I incorporated dedicated storage for stemmed glassware beneath the upper section of the bar.

Using a **wood router**, I created shaped openings in the wooden supports to accommodate the bases and stems of martini glasses and similar stemware.

The arrangement allowed glasses to be stored upside down.

This provided several practical benefits:

- Made use of otherwise underutilized overhead space.
- Kept the main shelves available for bottles and accessories.
- Allowed individual glasses to be removed when needed.
- Integrated a functional storage feature directly into the wooden structure.

The routing required consideration of the glassware geometry, spacing, and the relationship between the glass bases and the supporting wood.

This aspect of the project demonstrates the use of woodworking equipment to create functional features rather than purely decorative details.

## 7. Red Mahogany Stain & Polyurethane Finishing

After completing the woodworking and assembly stages, I applied a **red mahogany stain and polyurethane finish** to the reclaimed timber.

The purpose was to achieve a consistent appearance across the reused wood while also providing a protective finished surface.

Because the timber originated from repurposed storage crates, achieving a cohesive furniture appearance was an important part of the project.

### Countertop Surface Refinement

The countertop received additional finishing attention.

After applying the finish, I **wet-sanded the top and used wood polish** to improve the surface texture and final appearance.

The finishing process helped produce the smoother, more reflective countertop visible in the completed project photographs.

This stage required attention to surface preparation, consistency, and the appearance of the final coating.

## 8. Integrated Storage & Glass Shelving

The completed bar incorporated several storage features within the main structure.

These included:

- Side compartments for bottles and accessories.
- A central removable wine-bottle organizer.
- Glass shelving for displayed bottles and glassware.
- Mirrored backing within the storage areas.
- Routed overhead storage for inverted stemware.

The combination of glass shelves, mirrored panels, and stained wood provided a more refined appearance while maintaining the bar's original practical purpose.

The diamond-shaped central wine storage created a distinctive geometric feature within the lower section.

Importantly, the integration of these elements did not eliminate the ability to separate major components when required.

## 9. Design Outcomes & Technical Lessons

The completed home bar successfully brought together several fabrication disciplines within a single independently designed project.

The primary outcome was a sturdy, functional piece of furniture that could also be disassembled into major sections if required.

Several important design considerations were demonstrated throughout the build.

### Design for Assembly & Disassembly

The structural base used permanent connections where rigidity was important, while other components relied on removable fasteners.

This allowed the bar to maintain structural integrity during use without requiring every component to be permanently attached.

### Material Resourcefulness

Reclaimed storage-crate timber was repurposed into functional furniture, demonstrating the value of retaining and reusing suitable materials.

### Functional Fabrication

Custom glass cutting, routed stemware holders, and a removable wine rack demonstrated the integration of practical storage requirements into the design.

### Surface Quality & Finishing

Red mahogany stain, polyurethane coating, wet sanding, and polishing transformed reclaimed timber into a cohesive finished product.

### Aesthetic Integration

Concealed fasteners, decorative moulding, mirrored backing, and glass shelving were used to create a finished appearance while retaining the project's modular construction philosophy.

## Project Summary

This project demonstrates my ability to independently develop and fabricate a functional product while balancing **structural strength, modular construction, material reuse, functionality, and appearance**.

Although the bar was created as a personal woodworking project rather than a formal engineering assignment, the design decisions reflect principles that are directly applicable to mechanical design and fabrication.

In particular, the project illustrates the importance of considering **how components are assembled, accessed, maintained, and eventually disassembled** during the initial design process.
