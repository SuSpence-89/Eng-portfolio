## Project Overview

I designed and built a **modular home bar using reclaimed wood from former storage crates** after gathering visual inspiration from photographs online. The overall appearance was influenced by those references, but I independently developed the layout, construction approach, and assembly method.

From the beginning, I had two priorities: the finished bar needed to be **sturdy during everyday use**, while remaining **modular enough to take apart into larger sections** if it needed to be moved.

## 1. Material Sourcing & Reuse

The wood used for this project came from **wooden LUU-2D/B storage crates** that I encountered while working in the **X-Area during an Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) assignment** in the Royal Canadian Air Force. I retained the timber for use in a future personal project.

Reusing wood is a long-standing part of how I approach making things. I tend to save usable material rather than purchase new lumber when existing stock will suit the purpose. For this build, the reclaimed boards became the starting material for a bar that was designed around practical function, storage, appearance, and eventual disassembly.

This approach reduced the need for new material and turned previously used crate timber into a useful piece of furniture.

## 2. Design Objectives & Planning

Rather than reproducing a purchased set of plans, I developed the bar's construction around the intended use, available material, storage needs, and future disassembly requirements.

The design called for a rigid foundation, enclosed side storage, a central wine-bottle organizer, and a continuous countertop. I also wanted visible fastening points to be concealed so the finished piece would look like a conventional built-in bar rather than a knock-down assembly.

## 3. Structural Base & Modular Construction

The **base was assembled using screws and glue** to create a strong, permanent foundation. I treated that base as one structural module rather than attempting to make every individual joint removable.

The remaining major elements were attached mechanically so they could be separated when necessary:

- **Side compartments:** Fastened with screws, with the screw locations concealed behind a removable cover.
- **Central wine-bottle organizer:** Designed as a removable insert, retained with only a small number of screws so it could be pulled out as a separate unit.
- **Countertop:** Secured with screws rather than permanently bonded to the other modules, allowing it to be detached as part of a future disassembly.

This approach deliberately balanced **rigidity and serviceability**: permanent joints were concentrated in the foundation, while larger sections were joined using removable fasteners.

## 4. Concealed Fasteners & Decorative Detailing

I wanted the bar to retain a finished, furniture-like appearance without giving up the option to take it apart. I therefore concealed the side-compartment screws with a **removable cover**.

The countertop fasteners were hidden using **Alexandria Moulding egg-and-dart decorative basswood moulding**, specified as **5/8 in × 1 in × 8 ft**. The moulding served as both a finishing detail and a way of disguising the underlying fastening arrangement.

The result was a modular design that did not prominently display the attachment hardware in normal use. Disassembly could require removing the appropriate cover or trim to access the screws; the design was intended for occasional relocation rather than daily tool-free assembly.

## 5. Integrated Storage & Finishing

The bar included side compartments and a **central removable wine-bottle storage section**. Separating the wine organizer from the main base allowed it to be handled as an individual assembly instead of being permanently integrated into the structure.

The wood surfaces were finished to produce a cohesive appearance while retaining visible variation in the reclaimed material.

## Design Outcome

The completed project demonstrates **resourceful material reuse** alongside **practical design for assembly and disassembly** in a furniture-scale fabrication project. By combining a permanently bonded and screwed base with removable fasteners in the upper and storage sections, I created a sturdy bar that could also be separated into manageable modules if required.

This was a hands-on, independently planned build rather than a formal structural engineering exercise. Its value as a portfolio project lies in the design choices, construction sequencing, use of mechanical fastening, and integration of maintenance and relocation considerations into the finished product.

### Skills Demonstrated

- Translating visual inspiration into an independent construction design.
- Balancing structural rigidity with modularity and future disassembly.
- Organizing a build into separate subassemblies.
- Selecting screw-fastened versus glued connections according to purpose.
- Concealing accessible fasteners without compromising the appearance.
- Repurposing previously used storage-crate timber instead of purchasing new lumber.
- Working with reclaimed timber, storage geometry, and decorative moulding.
- Completing a functional piece of furniture with an integrated finish.
