# Portable Electric Hydraulic Power Unit

**Mechanical design · Hydraulic systems · SolidWorks · Design for fabrication**

*Developing a practical electric alternative to the gasoline-powered hydraulic unit used by field-service technicians.*

## The challenge

Shop technicians performing work at off-site locations had been relying on a gasoline-powered hydraulic power unit. The assignment was to investigate a portable, electrically driven alternative that could provide the required hydraulic capabilities while accommodating the connections and controls technicians used in the field.

This was more than a motor-and-pump selection exercise: the unit also needed an effective arrangement for its reservoir, hydraulic controls, hoses, electrical equipment, and supporting structure.

## My role

As a **Mechanical Technologist**, I contributed to hydraulic system research and component selection, preliminary sizing calculations, SolidWorks modelling, mechanical layout, and manufacturing documentation. I also worked with electrical engineers to identify a suitable electrical disconnect arrangement and coordinate its integration with the mechanical design.

## From requirements to a workable layout

**Hydraulic sizing and selection.** The concept began with a smaller electric drive package. As I investigated the required flow and maximum-pressure conditions, the selection evolved toward a larger motor and two-stage pump arrangement. The design work involved comparing operating conditions, checking power requirements, and reviewing hydraulic component compatibility.

**Controls and connections.** I developed a top-mounted control-manifold arrangement incorporating manually operated directional control valves, a pressure gauge, and hydraulic connection points. The layout emphasized clear operator access and practical routing of hoses and fittings.

**Structural design and mobility.** The later frame revision moved to a more open arrangement, with a square upper support frame on a longer rectangular base. This created room for the reservoir and motor/pump assembly while reserving a separate area for a small tool chest used to organize fittings and accessories. Swivel casters and forklift pockets were incorporated as handling considerations.

**Hose storage and fluid containment.** Square U-bolt-style hangers provided dedicated storage for additional hydraulic hoses. A welded rectangular hollow structural section (HSS) lip was added around the base to help retain small oil leaks or drips on the deck rather than allowing them to run directly onto the floor.

**Electrical coordination.** I coordinated with electrical engineers on the suitability and physical mounting of the electrical disconnect, ensuring the mechanical layout allowed for the enclosure and associated cabling.

## Design development

The project progressed through multiple layout revisions. The later configuration addressed several practical considerations that are easy to overlook in a CAD-only design:

- Access to valve controls, pressure indication, and hydraulic quick connections.
- Placement and mounting of the motor, pump, reservoir, and manifold.
- Storage for hoses, fittings, and technician tools.
- Shop handling and movement between work areas.
- Spill management and serviceability.
- Coordination of mechanical and electrical interfaces.

I used the SolidWorks assembly to work through spatial relationships and support the production of assembly details, fabrication drawings, and supporting parts documentation.

## What this project demonstrates

The most valuable part of this project was translating an operational request into a mechanical design that considered **hydraulic function, manufacturability, maintenance access, and the people who would use the equipment**.

It demonstrates how I approach engineering technology work: investigate the requirements, calculate and compare options, model the arrangement, refine it through review, and document the design clearly enough for others to evaluate and build.
