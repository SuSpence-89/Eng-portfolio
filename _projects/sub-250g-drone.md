## Project Overview

This project involved the design and development of a **3D-printable drone chassis** intended for a lightweight multirotor platform. The work focused on moving from **early concept selection**, into **CAD development**, and then into **slicing and print preparation** for fabrication.

Rather than jumping straight into one design, I explored multiple layout concepts first. This allowed me to compare overall geometry, symmetry, propeller protection, and structural simplicity before investing more time in detailed modelling.

The result was a structured design process that reflected how I prefer to work: **evaluate concepts first, refine a selected direction in CAD, and then assess manufacturability through slicing before printing**.

---

## 1. Design Intent

The chassis was developed as a **symmetry-driven drone frame** with integrated **full-height propeller hoops**. My goal was to create a layout that balanced:

- **lightweight construction**
- **printability**
- **structural continuity**
- **protected propeller geometry**
- **visually clean and repeatable symmetry**

Because the chassis was intended for additive manufacturing, the design also had to consider practical printing constraints such as part orientation, support generation, print duration, and total material use.

---

## 2. Concept Selection

Before settling on one configuration, I compared several candidate layouts. These included different rotor counts and symmetry patterns, with attention given to how each configuration might influence the overall structure and printable form.

The concepts shown below illustrate the type of comparison I carried out early in the design stage:

- **Fourfold Quad**
- **Sixfold Hex**
- **Hybrid Rosette Quad**

This stage helped me evaluate the trade-offs between rotor arrangement, central hub complexity, and the relationship between the motor hoops and the connecting structural members.

---

## 3. CAD Development

After selecting a preferred concept, I developed the chassis in CAD. This phase involved converting the chosen geometry into a manufacturable model with:

- a **central hub**
- **curved connecting arms**
- **full-height propeller hoops**
- internal support members within each rotor shroud
- mounting and interface features in the centre structure

The CAD stage was where the concept became a workable prototype. This allowed me to assess the overall form, inspect clearances, and refine how the outer hoops connected back into the central hub.

This phase was especially valuable because it made it possible to judge whether the design was not only visually appealing, but also realistic to print and assemble.

---

## 4. Slicing & Print Preparation

Once the CAD model was prepared, I moved into the slicing stage to evaluate the design as a real printable part.

Using slicer software, I reviewed:

- part orientation on the print bed
- support requirements
- estimated print time
- material consumption
- layer-by-layer manufacturability

The slicing preview confirmed that the chassis could be produced as a single printed part and provided useful feedback on the practicality of the selected geometry.

This step was important because it linked the digital model to the physical production process. A design that looks good in CAD still has to be printable in a reliable and efficient way, and the slicer preview provided that check.

---

## 5. Engineering Value of the Project

Although this was a personal project, it reflects several engineering habits that are directly transferable to professional work:

- starting with **multiple candidate concepts**
- selecting a preferred design based on geometry and practicality
- developing a concept into a functional CAD model
- considering **manufacturability early**
- using print preparation as part of the design validation process

This project also demonstrates a design mindset that values **iteration**, **visual comparison**, and **practical fabrication constraints**, rather than treating CAD as a purely aesthetic exercise.

## Design Outcome

The drone chassis project demonstrates a complete miniature workflow from **concept generation**, to **concept selection**, to **CAD development**, and finally to **print preparation**.

Even at the prototype stage, the project highlights the importance of designing with manufacturing in mind. It also shows my interest in combining **mechanical design**, **symmetry-driven geometry**, and **3D printing** into practical prototype development.
