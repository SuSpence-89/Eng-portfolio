## Project Overview

As an **Aviation Systems Technician** at the **Aerospace Engineering Test Establishment (AETE)** in Cold Lake, Alberta, I supported first-line maintenance and ground-servicing activities involving **CT-114 Tutor** and **CF-188 Hornet** aircraft. My responsibilities combined practical mechanical maintenance, aircraft handling, equipment readiness, technical documentation, and the supervision of developing technicians.

Working in an aviation environment where aircraft configuration, traceability, and safety were critical, I applied approved technical instructions, local procedures, and task-specific maintenance responsibilities. Over time, my work expanded from supporting servicing activities to **instructing and supervising other technicians** during mechanical maintenance and ground operations.

## 1. CT-114 Tutor Mechanical Maintenance

Following specialized CT-114 Tutor type training in 2016, I worked with the Tutor maintenance team on aircraft mechanical systems and first-line maintenance tasks.

My documented responsibilities included instructing or supervising technicians during work involving:

- **Nose landing gear actuators** and related landing-gear components.
- **Starter-generator removal and installation**, with attention to prescribed maintenance procedures.
- **Bleed-air valve installation** and component handling.
- **Left- and right-hand landing-gear oleo assemblies**.
- **Aircraft brake removal and installation**.
- **External fuel tank installation** and associated servicing tasks.
- **Engine-area access and clamshell removal/installation**, including practical guidance for less-experienced technicians.

These activities required interpreting technical instructions, communicating task requirements, checking work at appropriate stages, and ensuring personnel understood both the mechanical work and the associated safety precautions. Where I acted as an instructor or supervisor, I remained responsible for working within the specific maintenance privileges and inspection arrangements applicable to the task.

## 2. CF-188 Hornet Servicing & Support

During my earlier assignment at AETE, I supported **CF-188 Hornet** servicing and ground operations as part of a maintenance team. This included assisting with aircraft movements, servicing equipment, and routine preparation activities while learning the aircraft's local operating and maintenance procedures.

I also contributed to the readiness of **aircraft maintenance support equipment (AMSE)**. Examples included performing daily inspections, updating inspection sheets to make requirements clearer for the crew, and arranging improved protective covers for equipment exposed to weather.

In one instance, I identified that a required aircraft wheel-related support plate was missing and coordinated a replacement through the appropriate shop. This illustrates the practical problem-solving and inter-shop coordination needed to maintain serviceable support equipment and avoid unnecessary delays.

## 3. Aircraft Ground Handling & Servicing Coordination

I assisted with aircraft towing and parking operations and later acted as a **servicing section lead (I/C)** for selected activities. Responsibilities included coordinating technicians, communicating ground-handling requirements, and supervising aircraft movements and routine servicing.

I also trained personnel in aircraft parking, start-up support, fueling practices, and related procedural requirements. My focus was on clear communication, positive control of the work area, and following approved ground-servicing procedures.

### Fueling Safety Intervention

While supervising a CT-114 fueling activity in 2018, I noticed a loose connection lever at a fueling interface as the operation was beginning. I **instructed the technician to stop**, addressed the connection issue, and ensured the situation was corrected before work continued. This intervention demonstrated the importance of observation and immediate action when a condition does not meet the expected standard.

## 4. Training, Mentorship & Technical Supervision

A significant part of my role was helping newer Aviation Systems Technicians progress from observing maintenance to performing approved tasks with greater confidence and consistency.

I provided practical instruction during component removal and installation, engine-access work, aircraft servicing, and ground handling. I emphasized the use of technical publications, correct work sequencing, safe tool use, and the importance of recognizing when an inspection or additional authorization was required.

In 2018, I completed a **Level A first-line maintenance competency interview** addressing the responsibilities associated with task-specific maintenance release privileges. Maintenance authorization was never a blanket approval for all aircraft or systems: the scope of a technician's authority depended on the particular aircraft, maintenance task, and authorization held.

## 5. Equipment Readiness & Process Improvement

In addition to direct aircraft maintenance, I identified and implemented small practical improvements that supported safer, more efficient work:

- **Ground equipment readiness:** Improved AMSE daily-inspection documentation and coordinated more suitable weather protection for equipment.
- **Aircraft servicing organization:** Encouraged consistent placement of aircraft ground-safety pins so that personnel could locate them readily when needed. My 2018 work records indicate that this became an established local practice.
- **Tool-control accuracy:** Identified a discrepancy in an equipment tracking record and followed up to have the relevant toolkit information corrected, supporting proper access and accountability.
- **Housekeeping and environmental care:** Supported organized work areas, equipment servicing, and the appropriate handling of routine shop materials and waste.

These improvements were modest individually but important in a maintenance environment where **equipment availability, configuration control, and consistent procedures** directly affect daily work.

## Technical Outcomes

My time supporting CT-114 and CF-188 operations at AETE strengthened my practical capabilities in **aircraft mechanical maintenance, first-line servicing, maintenance safety, equipment readiness, technical instruction, and supervision**. It also expanded my experience coordinating work across teams and recognizing the relationship between a well-controlled maintenance process and aircraft operational readiness.

The most valuable development was progressing beyond individual task performance to helping **other technicians complete work safely, consistently, and in accordance with their authorized maintenance responsibilities**.
