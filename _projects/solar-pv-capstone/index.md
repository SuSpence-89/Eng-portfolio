**Lakeland College | Sustainable Energy Technology | June 2025**

**Location:** Vermilion, Alberta  
**Project Type:** Academic Capstone — Practical Field Experience  
**Focus:** Photovoltaic installation, electrical integration, solar tracking systems, energy storage and monitoring

## Project Overview

As the practical culmination of my Sustainable Energy Technology studies at Lakeland College, I participated in a week-long capstone focused on photovoltaic systems and the practical application of renewable energy technologies.

Working alongside fellow students, I gained hands-on experience in solar site assessment, system inspection, mechanical installation, electrical integration, troubleshooting, energy storage and performance monitoring.

The capstone provided an opportunity to connect the theoretical knowledge developed throughout the program with the challenges of working on real photovoltaic equipment and installations.

## 1. Solar Resource Assessment & System Planning

My academic studies included solar irradiance assessment, sun-path analysis, array orientation, photovoltaic system sizing, equipment selection and electrical installation principles.

These concepts were reinforced during the capstone through practical site assessment and examination of operating solar energy systems.

An important part of the experience was understanding how environmental conditions, equipment configuration, physical installation constraints and electrical requirements influence photovoltaic system performance.

## 2. Residential-Style Rooftop PV Installation

Following fall-arrest training, our team performed a photovoltaic installation on a purpose-built mock roof approximately six feet above ground level.

The structure replicated the characteristics of residential sheet-metal roofing, allowing us to complete the practical installation process in a controlled training environment.

I participated directly in:

- Planning photovoltaic module and racking layouts.
- Establishing clearances and setbacks from roof edges.
- Positioning and installing mounting rails and hardware.
- Mounting and securing photovoltaic modules.
- Routing and securing associated electrical cabling.
- Connecting dedicated module-level microinverters and their associated wiring.

The installation emphasized the importance of proper layout, roof accessibility, mechanical attachment, cable management and electrical integration.

### Rooftop Setbacks & Safety Considerations

The mock roof was designed to reinforce realistic installation requirements, including roof-edge clearances, access pathways and safe working practices.

Our training addressed applicable building and occupational safety considerations, demonstrating that successful PV installations must account for more than module placement and electrical performance.

## 3. Solar Tracking System Troubleshooting & Restoration

One of the most technically valuable activities involved inspecting and restoring an electromechanical solar tracking array that had been damaged during a weather event.

The damage included tangled, severed and damaged cabling and electrical connections associated with the tracking equipment.

With the system de-energized, our team worked to identify the affected connections, restore the damaged cabling and return the tracking system to operation.

Following the work, we verified its operation using the monitoring system located in another building.

This exercise provided practical experience in systematic troubleshooting, electrical isolation, equipment maintenance and electromechanical system verification.

## 4. Battery Storage & Electrical Monitoring

The capstone also included inspection and evaluation of battery-backed photovoltaic equipment.

We examined inverter and charge-control equipment, battery banks, monitoring interfaces and historical operating records.

I independently performed battery-voltage measurements to assess charging conditions and reviewed recorded battery-voltage trends.

These activities helped connect photovoltaic generation, electrical demand, battery charging and long-term energy storage performance.

## 5. Electrical Safety, Inspection & Permitting

Our training incorporated electrical inspections, Canadian electrical installation principles, permitting procedures and the responsibilities associated with grid-connected photovoltaic systems.

We reviewed the process of obtaining electrical permits and the distinction between work performed during supervised technical training and work requiring appropriately qualified electrical personnel.

Although our team participated in the practical installation and integration activities, we did not perform the final connection to the utility grid.

This reinforced the importance of regulatory compliance, electrical safety and recognizing the boundaries of professional qualifications.

## 6. Technical Outcomes & Lessons Learned

This capstone brought together several aspects of renewable energy technology into one practical learning experience.

Key outcomes included hands-on photovoltaic mounting and microinverter installation, participation in the restoration of a weather-damaged solar tracking system, independent battery-voltage testing, and practical exposure to electrical inspection and system monitoring.

Most importantly, the experience reinforced my understanding of how mechanical structures, electrical equipment, energy storage and control systems must work together in reliable renewable energy installations.

It also demonstrated the importance of troubleshooting, teamwork, safety and practical decision-making when transitioning from classroom theory to field applications.
