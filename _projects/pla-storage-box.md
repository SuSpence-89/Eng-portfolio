## Project Overview

As part of recommissioning my Creality CR-10 S4 large-format FDM 3D printer, I completed a practical fabrication exercise involving a two-piece rectangular storage container with a removable lid.

The project provided an opportunity to apply slicer configuration, print preparation, material deposition, and post-print inspection techniques while establishing a functional printing workflow.

This was one of my first completed multi-component prints following the printer's recommissioning and preceded my more complex dragon egg prototype development.

## 1. Print Preparation & Configuration

The storage box model was prepared for fabrication using Ultimaker Cura.

During print preparation, I evaluated the model's physical dimensions, estimated print duration, and machine settings.

The container proved considerably larger than initially anticipated, reinforcing the importance of verifying model scale and dimensional requirements before beginning fabrication.

Cura indicated an estimated print duration of approximately three hours for the print job being evaluated.

I deliberately selected a slower printing speed to prioritize controlled material deposition during the early stages of recommissioning the printer.

These activities provided practical experience in balancing print quality, fabrication time, and machine operating parameters.

## 2. Fabrication & Component Inspection

Using the Creality CR-10 S4, I successfully produced both components:

- A rectangular, open-top storage container.
- A separate removable lid featuring an integrated raised handle.

The completed components were manufactured in red filament using the FDM process.

Visual inspection showed reasonably consistent exterior walls, recognizable geometric features, and the characteristic layer patterns associated with fused deposition modelling.

The successful fabrication of both components demonstrated the printer's ability to produce practical multi-part objects.

## 3. Functional Fit & Dimensional Evaluation

Following fabrication, I performed a practical fit assessment of the completed storage box and its removable lid.

The lid seated successfully on the container, confirming that the mating geometry allowed the components to assemble as intended.

However, the connection provided insufficient frictional retention. When the assembled container was inverted, the lid separated from the box under its own weight.

Based on the observed clearance, I identified a potential dimensional adjustment of approximately 0.3–0.5 mm to improve the fit between the mating components.

Possible contributing factors include print shrinkage, original model clearances, and dimensional variation during fabrication. Further measurements would be required to determine the actual cause.

The proposed dimensional adjustment has not yet been incorporated into a revised print.

This evaluation reinforced the importance of distinguishing successful component fabrication from functional assembly performance.

## 4. Technical Outcomes & Lessons Learned

The project demonstrated several practical aspects of additive manufacturing, including:

- Preparing and configuring models for FDM fabrication.
- Evaluating component scale and print-time estimates.
- Selecting conservative print parameters.
- Producing separate components intended to function together.
- Inspecting finished print quality.
- Evaluating assembly fit and dimensional clearances.
- Identifying opportunities for iterative improvement.

Although both components were successfully manufactured and assembled, the fit assessment identified an opportunity to improve mechanical retention through tighter dimensional control.

## 5. Future Improvements

Further development will focus on measuring the container and lid dimensions, evaluating their mating clearances, and determining the appropriate dimensional adjustment required to introduce a light friction fit.

A revised print would allow the proposed clearance adjustment to be evaluated through repeatable assembly and retention testing.

Additional documentation could include actual print duration, filament consumption, final dimensions, and comparison against the original CAD geometry.

**Project Status:** Both components successfully printed and test-fitted. The lid seats properly but requires improved frictional retention. Dimensional refinement has been identified for future testing.
