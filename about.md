# About Me

### There's More to My Story Than Engineering

Welcome! I'm Spencer Holmes.

While much of this website showcases my professional experience, engineering projects, and technical interests, I wanted to create a separate space that tells a little more about who I am beyond my work.

My journey has been anything but conventional.

# My Story

## Chapter 1: Growing Up on the Farm

### An Unconventional Childhood

I was born in British Columbia and grew up on a **150-acre hobby farm near Rock Creek, B.C.**, about as close to the Canada–United States border as you could possibly get.

I come from a large family of eight children. I'm the fifth-born and the youngest of the boys, which made for an interesting childhood, to say the least.

Hard work and the skilled trades have always been part of my family's history. My grandfather was an ironworker, my father followed in his footsteps, and two of my brothers also entered the trade.

**You could say ironworking runs in our blood.**

Growing up, my dad frequently worked away from home, which meant he wasn't always around for the day-to-day activities of farm life.

With eight children and a property full of animals, there was certainly never a shortage of things happening around the house.

And when I say we had animals, I mean we had **a lot of animals**.

Our hobby farm was home to the usual assortment of chickens, pigs, sheep, cows, and horses. But we also had some less conventional residents, including a llama, peacocks, and turkeys.

At one point, we even had a great horned owl.

It wasn't exactly your typical childhood, but it was the environment in which I grew up.

### Chores Before Breakfast

Growing up on a hobby farm meant there was always something that needed doing. With eight children and a variety of animals to look after, everyone had responsibilities.

One thing was certain: **chores came before breakfast.**

When I was younger, my primary responsibility was looking after the chickens. Every morning, I'd head out to collect the eggs, replenish their feed, and make sure they had fresh water.

As I got older, my responsibilities expanded to include the ducks and geese.

By the time I was around 12 to 15 years old, I'd graduated to looking after some of the larger animals, particularly the cows and pigs.

These chores required considerably more physical strength.

I remember having to toss **40-pound bales of hay over a five-foot-tall page-wire fence**.

Then there was the task of hauling water to the pigs.

The pigpen was approximately **285 metres away**, and I'd carry five-to-seven-gallon buckets of water there by hand. That meant a fairly substantial walk with a heavy bucket, followed by the return trip.

Looking back, I'm pretty sure all that water hauling had something to do with the forearm muscles I developed growing up!

But life wasn't all work.

Once the morning chores were finished, our days were a mixture of helping Mom around the house and property and finding ways to have fun with my siblings.

With eight children growing up together on a large farm, there was certainly no shortage of company or opportunities to entertain ourselves.

## Homeschooling — From Reluctant Reader to Bookworm

My siblings and I were all homeschooled, although the four youngest of us experienced it the most.

With my mother attempting to educate eight children while managing everything else that came with raising such a large family, there were inevitably gaps in our education.

In my case, one of those gaps was reading.

As a child, I simply refused to learn. It wasn't that I couldn't do it; I just had no interest in putting in the effort.

That changed when I was around 12 years old.

I began experiencing the social consequences of not being able to read. I remember situations where an instructor would ask someone to read something written on the board, and I couldn't.

It was embarrassing, especially when I realized other kids my age could do something I couldn't.

Peer pressure turned out to be a powerful motivator. It finally gave me the kick in the ass I needed to apply myself.

And once I started reading, something unexpected happened.

**I couldn't stop.**

It was almost as though years of missed reading suddenly caught up with me. I developed an enormous appetite for books, and before long, I was actually getting in trouble for reading *too much*.

I remember walking around with a book in my hands, reading as I went.

At night, long after I was supposed to be asleep, I'd be hiding under my blankets with a flashlight and whatever book I couldn't bring myself to put down.

I went from being unable to read at 12 to catching up remarkably quickly and eventually reading at an advanced level.

### The Books That Started It All

My breakthrough came with *Bob Books*. Something about those simple books finally made reading click for me.

From there, I discovered the *Animorphs* series, followed by R.L. Stine's *Goosebumps* and *Fear Street* books.

My first full-length novel was *Harry Potter*, which opened the door to a much bigger world of reading.

Before long, I found myself drawn increasingly toward fantasy literature.

I discovered authors such as:

- Piers Anthony
- Terry Brooks
- Terry Goodkind
- L.E. Modesitt Jr.
- Robert Jordan, particularly his *Wheel of Time* series

What started as a struggle to learn something as fundamental as reading eventually became one of my favourite pastimes.

Looking back, I find it amusing that something I stubbornly refused to do for so many years eventually became something I couldn't get enough of.

**I went from avoiding books entirely to sneaking around with a flashlight just to squeeze in a few more chapters.**

## The Books That Stayed With Me

As my love of reading grew, I found myself increasingly drawn to fantasy novels, particularly Terry Goodkind's *Sword of Truth* series.

By the time I reached *Faith of the Fallen*, the sixth novel in the series, I had become quite attached to its main protagonist, Richard Rahl.

What I particularly appreciated about these books was that they offered more than an entertaining story.

Each seemed to introduce a philosophical lesson or an interesting way of looking at the world.

Goodkind's **Wizard's Rules** were particularly memorable.

The First Rule explored how easily people can be deceived by what they want or fear to be true.

The Sixth Rule emphasized the importance of reason, independent thought, and examining reality rather than blindly accepting what we're told.

But it wasn't just the Wizard's Rules that stuck with me.

### Pride in Your Work

One passage from *Faith of the Fallen* that I particularly appreciated involved Richard repairing a dangerous, deteriorating staircase in the building where he was living.

Rather than simply accepting the situation or waiting for someone else to fix it, Richard took it upon himself to make things better.

What resonated with me wasn't simply that Richard repaired a dangerous staircase.

**It was the philosophy behind his decision.**

He recognized something needed to be done, took responsibility for improving it, and found personal satisfaction in doing the work properly.

To me, there's something important about taking ownership of your surroundings and having pride in what you accomplish, regardless of whether anyone expects or rewards you for it.

It wasn't about doing something extraordinary.

It was about doing something worthwhile because it needed to be done, and taking pride in doing it properly.

Those kinds of moments are what made the series memorable to me.

Of all the novels I've read over the years, **Terry Goodkind's *Faith of the Fallen* remains my favourite book of all time.**
